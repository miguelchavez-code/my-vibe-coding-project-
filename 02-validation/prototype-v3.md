## What it tests

_Tie it back to the validation brief: which assumption does this prototype put in front of a user?_

If the CRM shows me what I need to do today and makes logging effortless, I’ll use it to manage my follow-ups instead of my spreadsheet.

## Context injected (no placeholders)

- **Real user quotes on screen:** “It's faster to keep my deals in a spreadsheet than to fight the CRM's eight required fields.” / “I never know where the thing I need lives. Every screen looks like a settings page.”
- **Domain metrics on screen:** My prototype shows follow-ups due and activities logged to help reps manage their daily work. The Pilot & Research screen also shows the current CRM’s baseline metrics: 11-minute deal updates against a 2-minute target, 18% weekly adoption, eight required fields, 63% shadow-spreadsheet reliance, and 2.1/5 satisfaction.

These baselines explain the problem—not the prototype’s results. To assess whether the solution works, the pilot would measure repeat weekly use, the share of follow-ups logged, and reduced spreadsheet reliance.

## Iteration log (v1 → v3)

| Version | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | Made monetary amounts more prominent and added a concise “Next:” action to each Today card. | Helps reps quickly see the opportunity’s value and understand what to do without opening details. |
| v2 | Added stage tags, deal-value ranges, follow-up type, and overdue filters that work with search. | Helps reps find relevant follow-ups without scanning the entire list or reverting to a spreadsheet. |
| v3 | Adapted cards for mobile and introduced screen-height-aware pagination instead of list scrolling. | Keeps follow-ups and actions within reach on desktop and phones, reducing navigation effort. |
